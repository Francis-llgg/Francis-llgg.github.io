Case study

From raw mathematics data to a reproducible benchmark

MathBench was built as a diagnostic benchmark rather than another leaderboard number. It separates theoretical knowledge from application ability across educational stages and languages, making model weaknesses traceable.

Benchmark design

01Five stages

Arithmetic, primary, middle, high-school and college mathematics.

02Theory + application

Foundational knowledge is evaluated separately from practical problem solving.

03Bilingual evaluation

Chinese and English variants reveal language-dependent performance.

04Circular evaluation

Answer choices are rotated to reduce position bias and lucky guesses.

My contribution

I worked end to end—from discovering raw data to delivering a labeled, runnable evaluation benchmark.

  1. 01

    Sourced heterogeneous mathematics data from AMC competitions, Gaokao and postgraduate entrance examinations, plus K–12 textbook theory, spanning conceptual knowledge and worked problems.

  2. 02

    Cleaned, deduplicated and normalized the collected material, standardizing question text, answer choices, solutions and bilingual formatting for reliable evaluation.

  3. 03

    Designed and applied a structured annotation taxonomy across educational stage, theory or application, subject area, topic, language and source, then quality-checked labels and samples.

  4. 04

    Integrated the curated benchmark into OpenCompass, completed dataset configuration, inference and result aggregation, and systematically evaluated 30+ open- and closed-source models.

Core findings

The cleaned data and structured labels turn a single model score into an interpretable capability profile.

MathBench makes model weaknesses traceable through a cleaned, labeled and reproducible evaluation pipeline—not just a single leaderboard score.
